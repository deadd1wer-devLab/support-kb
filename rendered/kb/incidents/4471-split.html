parser · the shared parser reads day-first dates month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first (asked of the platform in locales.ts, not typed in a list). The shared parser reads every date month first, so a day-first date comes out with day and month swapped: “Booked for 2 March. The invite says 3 February.” This covers reports from the app, from email, and from the booking-v2 form. Fix: parse with the tenant’s locale. This is the fix #4471 already queued.

hint · booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field

Day-first tenants who run booking-v3 show their people a month-first hint. People type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, so the date flips before the parser sees it. Only reports that came in through the form, from booking-v3 tenants, are this problem. The parser fix does not touch it. Fix the hint (or read the field the way the hint asks), in booking-v3.

shift · the date moves by one day

The desk already marked these in its notes as “read as a one-day move”, then filed them under parser as a footnote. They are not a swap. “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” The day changes with where the invite is read, which points at a date sent as midnight and shown in another time zone. Neither the parser fix nor the hint fix reaches it.

Read the cause from the tenant, not the intake tag

Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser the moment it arrives. The “intake tagged” column above shows how often that guess disagrees with the tenant’s locale and form. Count by tenant.

Why one ticket hid three

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone, and reporters describe all three problems with the same words: “wrong day”, “date is wrong”. Same subject, different causes. The decision stays as accepted; a new record should replace it with a rule that keys on cause (tenant, form, channel), as the triage explorer already computes.
